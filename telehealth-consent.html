Telehealth Informed Consent

Last updated: October 9, 2026

Do not use this service if you may be experiencing a medical emergency. Call 911, go to the nearest emergency room, contact your local crisis center, or call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline).

This Telehealth Informed Consent describes what it means to receive care through telehealth on the Zevia Rx platform. By checking the agreement box in our intake and using the Services, you acknowledge and consent to the following. You may withdraw consent at any time by stopping use of the Services and notifying support@zeviarx.com — withdrawal will not affect care already delivered.

1. What telehealth is

Telehealth means receiving healthcare services remotely using electronic communications — here, a structured health intake reviewed asynchronously by an independent, licensed provider in your state, with follow-up by secure message, and, where required by your state or by clinical judgment, synchronous or recorded video interaction. Your provider may determine that telehealth is not appropriate for you and refer you to in-person care.

2. The provider relationship

The providers who review your intake are independent, board-certified, licensed clinicians, not employees of Zevia Rx. They exercise independent medical judgment, are licensed in the state where you are located, and are solely responsible for all clinical decisions — including whether to prescribe any medication. Purchasing a plan does not guarantee a prescription. If your provider determines treatment is not appropriate, your medication cost and shipping fee are refunded under our Refund & Cancellation Policy; the provider consultation fee is non-refundable.

3. Benefits and risks

Benefits of telehealth include convenient access to licensed clinicians without travel or scheduling delays. Risks include: your provider does not perform a physical examination and relies on the information you provide, so incomplete or inaccurate answers can lead to inappropriate care; delays can occur due to technology failures; and, despite safeguards, electronic transmission of information carries security risks (see our Privacy Policy). Alternatives to telehealth are available to you at any time, including in-person visits with your own physician.

4. GLP-1 treatment and compounded medication consent

"GLP-1 medications" means FDA-approved, commercially manufactured GLP-1 receptor agonists and compounded GLP-1 formulations prepared by a state-licensed compounding pharmacy (503A) or FDA-registered outsourcing facility (503B) when clinically appropriate. GLP-1 receptor agonists increase insulin secretion, reduce glucagon release, slow stomach emptying and promote fullness. They are intended to be used together with nutrition, exercise and lifestyle changes. Individual results vary and no specific outcome is guaranteed.

Risks. GLP-1 medications carry a boxed warning: studies have shown an association between GLP-1 receptor agonists and thyroid C-cell tumors (a causal relationship has not been established in humans), and they are not appropriate for people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2. Other potential risks include, but are not limited to:

  • Pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Kidney injury secondary to dehydration
  • Worsening of diabetic retinopathy
  • Severe gastrointestinal symptoms
  • Hypoglycemia, especially when combined with other glucose-lowering medicines

Side effects. Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, reflux, abdominal discomfort and fatigue. Less common or rare side effects include depression or mood changes, hair thinning, pancreatitis, gallbladder complications, severe allergic reactions and non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). You agree to report concerning symptoms to your provider promptly.

Do not use GLP-1 medications if you have or have had any of the following, and tell your provider about any change in your health: eating disorders; severe gastrointestinal disease (for example gastroparesis) unless cleared by a provider; chronic or prior pancreatitis; a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2); active gallbladder disease unless cleared by a provider; pregnancy or intent to become pregnant; breastfeeding. You agree to provide complete and accurate medical information.

Stop the medication and seek immediate medical attention if you experience severe or persistent abdominal pain, symptoms of pancreatitis or gallbladder disease, yellowing of the skin or eyes, sudden vision changes, decreased urination or swelling of the legs, chest pain or shortness of breath, or signs of an allergic reaction (swelling, difficulty breathing).

Compounded medication. Your provider decides whether an FDA-approved or compounded formulation is appropriate based on clinical judgment, availability, medical necessity and applicable law. If a compounded GLP-1 medication is prescribed, you understand that compounded medications are not FDA-approved for safety, effectiveness or quality in the same manner as commercially manufactured drugs and do not undergo FDA review; that they may differ in concentration, inactive ingredients (our injectable formulations contain glycine and the preservative benzyl alcohol), delivery system or preparation method; and that they are prepared pursuant to a patient-specific prescription by a licensed pharmacy under state and federal compounding regulations.

Report side effects to your provider promptly; for emergencies call 911; adverse events can also be reported to support@zeviarx.com and FDA MedWatch.

5. Identity verification and recordings

Telehealth law requires verification of your identity. You agree to provide a government-issued photo ID and, in states that require it, a short self-recorded video confirming your identity and consent to treatment. These materials are stored securely as part of your care record.

6. Your rights and acknowledgements

You have the right to: ask questions about your care and treatment options; refuse or discontinue treatment at any time; request a copy of your medical records (provided at the reasonable cost of preparation and delivery); know your provider's name, credentials and licensure; and receive care without discrimination. You understand that telehealth does not replace the relationship with your primary care doctor; that your provider decides whether your clinical needs are appropriate for telehealth; that technical failures can delay evaluation or lose information and you hold Zevia Rx and your providers harmless for such delays; that your provider may consult other clinicians about your care under the same confidentiality obligations; that no narcotics or controlled substances will be prescribed; and that there is no guarantee you will receive a prescription. Your medical information is handled as described in our Privacy Policy and your providers' own privacy practices, and is protected by applicable federal and state law.
